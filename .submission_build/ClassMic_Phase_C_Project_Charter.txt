1 Project purpose and authorization
ClassMic will test whether a student's own phone can work as a secure, instructor-controlled wireless microphone in a classroom. Students join a temporary browser session by QR code, request permission to speak, and use push to talk; only one approved phone transmits audio to the classroom sound system at a time.
This charter authorizes a limited pilot, not a campus-wide rollout. The project aims to make student questions easier to hear without passing a physical microphone, while protecting privacy through explicit microphone permission, no recording, no persistent audio storage, and instructor control.
2 SMART objective
Element | ClassMic response
Specific | Develop and pilot an instructor-controlled, browser-based system that lets students use a phone as a push-to-talk classroom microphone in three equipped classrooms.
Measurable | Success requires 90% of sessions to start within 2 minutes, 90% of approved requests to become audible within 3 seconds, median delay of 700 ms or less, 75% positive audibility feedback, and zero critical privacy or security incidents.
Achievable | The limited pilot reuses student phones, existing Wi-Fi, classroom speakers, and standard browsers, while one active microphone and a physical-mic fallback keep the solution realistic for the team and available resources.
Relevant | ClassMic supports inclusive participation and teaching quality by helping the whole room hear student questions without disrupting discussion to pass around a physical microphone.
Time-bound | The project will complete discovery, prototype development, testing, and a six-week classroom pilot within 14 weeks, followed by the Dean's decision to close, revise, or continue.
3 Scope and boundaries
In scope | Out of scope
Browser-based QR join; temporary classroom session; request-to-speak queue; instructor approve, mute, and end controls; one active microphone; push to talk; classroom speaker output; basic support guide; three-classroom pilot and evaluation. | Continuous or secret recording; saved audio or transcripts; voice identification; attendance or grading from speech; simultaneous student microphones; remote-learning use; campus-wide production deployment; permanent student profiles.

10 Constraints assumptions and principal risks
Constraints. The pilot must remain within 14 weeks, three equipped classrooms, and AED 60,000; use supported student browsers; provide a physical microphone or instructor-repeat fallback; and store no audio or transcript. The authorization ceiling is not an itemized cost baseline.
Assumptions. Pilot rooms have stable Wi-Fi and a computer connected to room speakers; most participants have a compatible phone; instructors will control the queue; IT and privacy specialists can review the pilot; and participants receive clear notice before microphone access.
Principal risks. Audio feedback, network delay, misuse, incompatible devices, low instructor adoption, and unequal phone access could reduce value. Controls include one active microphone, push to talk, pre-pilot room tests, instructor mute and end controls, a loaner or physical-mic fallback, training, and incident procedures.
