1 Integrated plan summary
This plan converts the approved ClassMic charter into a controlled 14-week delivery baseline. The team will define and build a browser-based, instructor-controlled phone microphone, validate it in three equipped classrooms, operate a six-week pilot, and present evidence for the Dean of Academic Affairs to close, revise, or continue the project.
Management focus. The critical management points are the privacy and architecture gate at the end of Week 3, classroom AV integration in Week 6, pilot-readiness approval at the end of Week 8, and support performance during the six-week pilot.
2 Scope statement
Product scope | Project scope
A temporary browser session reached by QR code; microphone permission notice; request-to-speak queue; instructor approval, mute, and end controls; one active push-to-talk microphone; room-speaker output; automatic session timeout; operational event logging without stored audio. | Confirm requirements; design and build the focused MVP; review privacy, security, accessibility, browser, network, and AV controls; train pilot instructors; run the three-classroom pilot; measure performance and adoption; provide a final recommendation.

Included in Release 1 | Excluded from Release 1
Three pilot classrooms; up to six instructors and 150 students; latest supported mobile browsers; basic instructor dashboard; user guidance; physical-microphone fallback; performance, support, and satisfaction measurement. | Continuous or secret recording; stored audio or transcripts; voice identification; attendance or grading from speech; simultaneous microphones; remote learning; permanent profiles; campus-wide rollout; production service-level commitments.
4 Constraints assumptions and dependencies
Type | Planning statement
Constraints | Finish within 14 weeks and AED 60,000; use three equipped classrooms; store no audio or transcript; retain a physical-microphone fallback; do not begin the live pilot with unresolved critical security, privacy, or safety defects.
Assumptions | Rooms have stable Wi-Fi and a computer connected to speakers; most participants have compatible phones; instructors will control the queue; IT, AV, accessibility, and privacy specialists can support planned reviews.

5 MVP and Release 1 priorities
Release 1 is the smallest complete classroom workflow that can test audibility, reliability, adoption, operational effort, and privacy controls. Optional features cannot delay the pilot-readiness gate.
Priority | Capabilities
Must have | QR or one-time-code join; explicit microphone permission; instructor session start and end; request queue; approve, mute, and remove; one active microphone; push to talk; room-speaker output; session timeout; no stored audio; basic event and incident logs; physical-mic fallback.
Should have | Pre-session device and network check; clear reconnect message; instructor view of connection health; anonymous end-of-session feedback; simple pilot metrics; short instructor and student help screens.
Could have | Ephemeral live captions after privacy approval; additional language cues; richer device diagnostics; configurable speaking timer; branded room display.
Will not have | Recording or transcript storage; voice recognition; attendance or grading; public profiles; simultaneous student microphones; remote-learning mode; external users; automated disciplinary decisions; campus-wide production deployment.
6 Priority user stories to prototype
ID | User story | Release 1 acceptance criteria
US1 | As an instructor, I want to start a temporary room session and display a QR code so students can join quickly. | Session and QR appear within 10 seconds; code expires when the instructor ends the session; a new session uses a different code.
US2 | As a student, I want to understand and grant microphone permission only when I choose to participate. | Purpose and no-recording notice appear before permission; denial leaves the student in listen-only status; permission can be revoked through the browser.
US3 | As a student, I want to request the microphone and use push to talk after approval so my question reaches the room speakers. | Request enters the queue once; approval is visibly confirmed; audio transmits only while push to talk is held; the microphone closes when released or timed out.
US4 | As an instructor, I want to see the queue and approve, mute, remove, or end speakers so discussion remains controlled. | Only one student can transmit; every control acts within 2 seconds under the test network; end session disconnects all phone microphones.
US5 | As a pilot owner, I want non-audio performance and support data so I can evaluate reliability without recording speech. | Logs contain session time, connection status, latency, failures, and control events; no audio or transcript is stored; the pilot summary reports agreed success measures.
7 Release 1 completion definition
Release 1 is complete only when all Must-have capabilities pass functional tests, the pilot-readiness quality thresholds are met, security and privacy owners approve the data flow, instructors can operate the workflow after the planned briefing, and no critical defect or risk exception remains unresolved.

14 Quality and acceptance approach
Quality means the Release 1 workflow is safe, usable, measurable, and reliable enough for a controlled pilot. Passing tests is necessary, but the business owner and control owners must also accept the evidence at the readiness gate.
Quality area | Acceptance criterion | Verification method | Acceptance owner
Setup and operability | At least 90% of observed sessions start within 2 minutes; instructor completes start approve mute and end without live help after briefing. | Timed scenario test and UAT observation | Business owner
Functional control | Only one student transmits; request approve mute remove timeout and end controls behave as specified; all Must-have tests pass. | Functional and integration test suite | Nurtore and Temiko
Performance and reliability | At least 90% of approved requests become audible within 3 seconds; median audio delay is 700 ms or less; at least 95% of pilot sessions finish without a ClassMic-caused stop. | Event logs, network test, and session log | Temiko and IT
Audio usefulness | Speech is intelligible in every pilot room at normal listening position, with no sustained feedback or unsafe volume. | Room checklist and instructor rating | AV lead and instructors
Privacy and security | No stored audio or transcript; codes expire; unauthorized users can be removed; access and retention controls pass review; no unresolved critical finding. | Data-flow review, configuration check, misuse test | Privacy and security owners
Compatibility and accessibility | Agreed iOS and Android browser matrix passes; denied permission has a clear recovery path; an equivalent physical or loaner microphone is always available. | Device matrix and accessibility review | Accessibility and IT leads
Stakeholder value | At least 75% of surveyed students and instructors say questions were easier to hear, and at least four of six instructors use ClassMic in two or more sessions. | Anonymous survey and usage evidence | Business owner
Defect threshold | Zero open critical defects; high defects resolved or explicitly accepted by the correct control owner and sponsor; medium defects have documented workarounds. | Defect review at Week 8 gate | Project manager

Procurement rule
No external provider may receive live audio, identifiers, or session data without documented security and privacy approval. Nurtore may place approved purchases already inside the cost baseline; a new vendor, commitment above AED 5,000, or any change that could store or process audio outside the institution requires Finance review and the Dean's approval.
