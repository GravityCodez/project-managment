PILOT SCOPE One campus • 500 invited students | TIME / FUNDING 12 weeks • AED 92,000 cap
RECOMMENDATION: GO — PILOT ONLY   Approve a 12-week custom MVP pilot capped at AED 92,000. Do not approve campus-wide production until local demand, safe adoption, and operating effort are measured against the decision gates on the final page.
1. Opportunity and proposed solution
The decision problem. Students can often see clubs and events, yet still struggle to turn interest into a first, low-pressure in-person interaction—especially when they are new, commute, have limited time, or do not already know an organizer. Existing channels advertise broadly; they do not reliably form a small group around a shared goal, time, and place.
CampusCrew. A verified, mobile-first campus service that creates temporary ‘crews’ of 3–6 students for a specific real-world activity: a study sprint, portfolio build, coding practice, sport, language exchange, or campus event. A 60-second intent card captures goal, availability, preferred language, and accessibility needs; the service proposes a suitable crew and public on-campus meetup. It has no infinite feed, follower counts, or public popularity metrics.
2. Why the case merits a pilot
Strategic alignment — strengthens belonging, peer learning, student experience, and cross-discipline collaboration while producing measurable participation data.
External need signal — WHO’s 2025 commission reported that roughly 17–21% of people aged 13–29 experience loneliness, and emphasized practical, scalable social-connection interventions [1].
Intervention logic — the U.S. Surgeon General recommends peer-led programs, supportive environments, and regular opportunities for connection in education settings [2]. CampusCrew operationalizes those principles around real activities.
Evidence caution — the U.S. Department of Education found mixed overall effects for college social-belonging interventions, although one commuter-heavy randomized study showed promising retention evidence [3]. This uncertainty supports a measured pilot, not a full rollout.

Option findings
1 — Improve current process (AED 28k–45k/year). Use existing email, forms, calendars, clubs, and messaging groups with a part-time coordinator. Fast and low-risk, but fragmented, manual, difficult to scale, and unlikely to solve the ‘first small group’ problem. Suitable only as a short baseline/control.
2 — Buy a commercial platform (AED 160k–300k Year 1 allowance). A product such as CampusGroups already offers groups, events, registration, attendance, analytics, mobile access, and integrations [4]. It is broad and mature, but public pricing is not provided; procurement, configuration, vendor dependency, and paying for unused modules reduce fit for a narrowly defined pilot. A supplier quote and privacy review would be mandatory.
3 — Build a focused custom MVP (AED 92k cap). Best fit to the differentiated workflow and smallest viable data footprint. It takes more design and technical ownership, but can reuse university identity, email/calendar, and analytics services. A rules-based matcher should be used first; optional AI may summarize free-text interests into tags, but must not make safety, disciplinary, or academic decisions.

6. Recommended pilot design
MVP must include | Explicitly out of scope
Verified SSO; optional intent card; transparent crew suggestions; organizer-confirmed public campus meetups; reminders; QR attendance; three-question pulse survey; reporting, moderation and incident workflow. | Public social feed; follower counts; open direct messages; external users; precise GPS tracking; health or academic-record data; paid events; autonomous AI decisions; ranking students by popularity or ‘fit’.
7. Principal risks and controls
Risk | Preventive / response control | Owner | Trigger
Low student adoption | Co-design with target users; ambassadors; seed useful crews; test onboarding weekly. | Product owner | <25% activation by day 14
Safety or misconduct | Verified accounts; public venues; code of conduct; report/block; escalation pathway. | Student Affairs | Any high-severity report
Privacy or breach | Consent, minimization, encryption, role access, logs, 90-day post-evaluation deletion. | Privacy / IT | Unauthorized access or loss
Biased or exclusionary matching | Rules first; explain matches; opt-out/edit preferences; accessibility and language testing. | Product owner | >15-point outcome gap
Support load exceeds value | Self-service help; triage queue; weekly defect review; cap cohorts. | Service owner | >0.25 FTE for 2 weeks
Privacy requirement. UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 provides a framework for personal-data governance and generally prohibits processing without consent except in specified cases [5]. The pilot must document purpose, lawful basis, consent, access rights, security controls, retention, deletion, and any processor arrangements before recruitment.
8. Success measures and decision gates
Measure | Pilot threshold | Evidence
Activation | ≥35% of 500 invitees | Verified onboarding log
Match acceptance | ≥50% of proposed crews | Accepted / proposed
Real-world participation | ≥75 unique attendees | QR check-in + audit sample
Connection outcome | ≥60% report one useful new connection | Anonymous post-meetup pulse
Speed | Median ≤7 days to accepted crew | Event timestamps
Safety / operations | 0 unresolved critical incidents; ≤0.25 FTE | Incident + support logs
GO to production discovery: zero unresolved critical incidents, the safety/operations gate is met, and at least four of the five engagement/outcome thresholds are achieved. REVISE: no critical incident, but only two or three thresholds are met; run one corrected cohort within the existing cap. NO-GO: fewer than two thresholds are met, support is disproportionate, or a severe control failure cannot be acceptably remediated.
DECISION   GO with Option 3: a tightly scoped custom MVP pilot. Preserve Option 1 as the comparison baseline; request a commercial quote only if the pilot proves demand for broader club, event, and engagement-management capabilities.
